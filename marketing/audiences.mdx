An audience is just a **saved contact list**. There is no separate audience builder.

<Info>
  **You build audiences on the Contacts screen, not in Marketing.**

  Filter your contacts however you need, save that list, and it appears in the audience picker the next time you send a blast. One place to build a segment, one segment model across the whole app, rather than two lists that drift apart.
</Info>

## Building one

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Contacts and filter">
    Use the filter rail to narrow to the people you want. Combine as many filters as you like.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the list">
    Save it with a clear, specific name. That name is what you will be picking from later, so "Industrial owners, north valley" beats "List 3".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use it in a blast">
    In **Marketing > New Blast**, your saved list is in the audience picker.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What is already in the picker

You do not have to build anything to send your first blast. The picker offers **All contacts**, then the built-in CRE segments, then your own saved lists.

The built-in segments are the same ones in your Contacts nav:

| | |
|---|---|
| Sellers · Hot Sellers | Landlords · Tenants |
| Cash buyers · Financed buyers | Past Clients |
| Brokers / Referral Partners | Dormant / Needs Reconnecting |
| This Week's New Leads · Recently Added | Cold Call Sheet |

<Note>
  **Your AI call lists are deliberately kept out of the blast picker.**

  When you ask AI Fuel to build you a call list, that is a money-priority list of people to **phone**. Those are not a mailing audience, and having them appear in the blast dropdown is how someone accidentally emails a list they meant to dial.

  They stay in Contacts where they belong. This is on purpose, not an omission.
</Note>

## Lists stay current

A saved list is a filter, not a frozen snapshot of names. If someone gains a tag or goes cold, they move in or out of the list on their own. You never rebuild it by hand.

<Tip>
  Check the recipient count before you send. It is the fastest way to catch a filter that is broader or narrower than you meant, and it costs two seconds.
</Tip>
