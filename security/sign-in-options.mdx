CRE Fuel accounts are invite-only. Whichever way you sign in, the email has to already belong to an account — having a valid Google or Microsoft account is not by itself a way in.

## Email and password

The standard way in. Your password is never stored in a readable form, by us or anyone else.

CRE Fuel remembers you on a device you have signed in on before, so you are not retyping it every morning. A new device asks again.

### Changing your password

There is no old-password-then-new-password form in CRE Fuel, and that is on purpose. A password is always set through a one-time link sent to your email, so nobody can change it without access to your inbox.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for the link">
    **Settings > Security > Email me a link to change my password.** Or, from the sign-in screen, use **Set my password**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the email">
    Click the link. It is single-use and expires.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the new password">
    Type it in and you are done.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  If the sign-in screen says no account uses that email, it means exactly that — the password is not the problem. Check the spelling, or whether you have a second work address.
</Tip>

## Continue with Google

If your brokerage runs on Google Workspace, you can sign in with the Google account you already use. Google checks who you are and tells CRE Fuel your verified email address.

CRE Fuel asks Google for your name and email address, and nothing else. It cannot read your Gmail, your calendar, or your contacts.

---

## Continue with Microsoft

If your brokerage runs on Microsoft 365, your IT team can switch on Microsoft sign-in. Same idea: Microsoft checks who you are, and CRE Fuel asks only for your name and email address. It cannot read your Outlook mail, your calendar, or your contacts.

This is usually what an IT department means when they ask whether CRE Fuel "supports SSO."

<Note>
  Microsoft sign-in has to be switched on by your IT team first, and the button only appears once it is. Your IT team controls it and can switch it off at any time without going through us. The setup detail is on [For Your IT Team](/security/for-your-it-team).
</Note>

### Why your IT team likes this

When someone leaves the brokerage, disabling their Microsoft or Google account closes their way into CRE Fuel at the same time. Nobody has to remember to go and remove a separate password.

---

## Two-factor applies to all of them

If you have [two-factor authentication](/security/two-factor) turned on, you will be asked for your 6-digit code no matter which of the three routes you use.

There is no door that skips it. A second step you could get around by clicking a different button would not be worth having.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I have to pick one?">
    No. If your account has a password and your organization has Google or Microsoft sign-in switched on, any of them work. Use whichever is in front of you.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I signed in with Google, do I still need a password?">
    Not for signing in. Plenty of brokers never set one. It is worth having as a backstop in case you ever cannot reach your Google account.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why does it say my account isn't finished being set up?">
    Your credentials were right, but the account has not been fully provisioned yet. Nothing you did — reach out and we will finish it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How long does CRE Fuel keep me signed in?">
    About 30 days on a device you have used, then it asks again. Signing out ends it immediately.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
