Qualifying a buyer records what they are looking for, so that when a listing lands you can find the right people in seconds instead of going from memory.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the contact and click Qualify as Buyer">
    On the contact record, click **Qualify as Buyer**. If they are already qualified the button reads **Edit qualification**, and a **Buyer ✓** marker shows on the record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the three required fields">
    **Tier**, **Buyer type**, and **Price range**. You cannot save without all three, and the dialog will tell you which one is missing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add whatever else you know">
    Asset class, location, cap rate. Partial is fine and normal. You will learn more on the next call.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Buyer type is INTENT, not financing

This is the field people get wrong, so it is worth being blunt about.

**Buyer type describes what the buyer wants out of the asset:**

| Buyer type | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Investment (Cap Rate)** | They are buying income. The return is the point. |
| **Owner-User** | They are buying somewhere to run their own business. |
| **Both** | They will look at either. |
| **None** | Not applicable. |

**Whether they pay cash is a completely separate question.** Cash versus financed is recorded as a **tier tag** (cash buyer / financed buyer), never as buyer type.

<Warning>
  Do not put "cash" in buyer type. A cash buyer might be an investor or an owner-user, and those two want opposite properties. Collapsing the two questions into one field throws away the answer that actually drives matching.
</Warning>

## Primary asset class drives their follow-up cadence

If you set a **primary asset class**, it is not just a label. **The follow-up cadence branches on it**, so an industrial buyer and a retail buyer can sit on different clocks.

<Tip>
  Set the primary class even for a buyer who looks at several things. It decides how often they hear from you, and "everything" is not a schedule.
</Tip>

## The rest of the buy box

- **Price range** — required, and displayed with thousands separators so a $3,000,000 buyer is never mistaken for a $30,000,000 one.
- **Asset class** — everything they will consider, which can be broader than their primary.
- **Location** — the markets or submarkets they will look at.
- **Cap rate from** — the minimum return an investment buyer will accept.

<Tip>
  **Copy for Fuel AI** puts the buyer's requirement on your clipboard as plain text, which is handy for pasting into an email or a text without retyping what they told you.
</Tip>

<Note>
  A partial buy box still matches. The more you fill in the tighter the results, but do not leave a half-known buyer unrecorded because you are missing their cap-rate floor.
</Note>
