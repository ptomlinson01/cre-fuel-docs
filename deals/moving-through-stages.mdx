Your pipeline is only useful if the stages are current. Every time something meaningful happens on a deal, move the stage forward. It takes about five seconds and keeps your scorecard and forecast accurate.

## The four starting stages

Every pipeline starts with the same four stages, on purpose. Sellers, Buyers and Tenants are different work, but "where is this?" should mean the same thing in each.

| Stage | What it means |
|---|---|
| **New** | The opportunity exists. You have not really started on it. |
| **Active** | You are working it. On the seller side this is a live, marketed listing. |
| **Under Contract** | Signed and in process. On the seller side, the executed PSA is the gate. |
| **Closed / Won** | The transaction completed. This is the one that counts. |

<Warning>
  **Winning the listing is not a Won.** Taking a listing moves the deal to **Active**, not to Closed/Won. Won means the transaction closed and there is commission to record.

  This is the single most common way a pipeline starts lying to you. Mark listings as Won and your close rate, your production numbers, and your Scorecard all overstate the year.
</Warning>

## Add your own steps

Four stages is where you start, not a limit. If you work in more steps, like a tour or a due diligence period, the account owner can add them in **Settings > Customization > Pipelines**. Each new stage says what it **counts as** (New, Active or Under Contract), so your numbers, Leads and deal cards keep working no matter what you call it. See [Pipelines settings](/settings/pipelines).

## Move a deal

The board is columns you drag between. Grab the card and drop it in the next column, and the stage updates.

The list view is the same deals in a different head: **the list is for working, the board is for seeing.**

<Note>
  A deal does not have to walk every stage. A transaction you are recording after the fact can go straight to Closed/Won without passing through New, Active and Under Contract.
</Note>

## Going under contract: when did escrow open?

When you move a deal into Under Contract (or any stage that counts as Under Contract), CRE Fuel asks one question: **when did escrow open?** Use the date on title's opening-of-escrow email. No escrow email? Use the date your contract's deadlines count from.

- **Pick the date** (it starts on today) and the deal moves.
- **Not open yet** moves the deal now and leaves the date blank. Signed Thursday, escrow opens Monday? Add it Monday.
- **Also set due diligence** fills the DD date that many days later (45 to start; CRE Fuel remembers the number you use). It only shows on a sale with no DD date yet.

On a lease, the question is when the lease was drafted, and there's no DD.

The date shows on the deal card as **Opened** (or **Drafted** on a lease), next to the closing date. Click it to change it. Adding it to a deal that's still New or Active moves the deal to Under Contract.

Why it matters: your escrow checklist, the Offers Accepted number on your Verdict, and your Deal Funnel all date the deal from this, not from the day you happened to drag the card. Leave it blank and they fall back to the day the deal moved.

<Note>
  Moving a deal to **Closed** triggers the commission entry step. CRE Fuel will prompt you to fill in closing details before the deal is marked complete. See [Closing and Commissions](/deals/closing-and-commissions) for the full walkthrough.
</Note>
