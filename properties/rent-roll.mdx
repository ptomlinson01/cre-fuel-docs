A rent roll is the list of every leasable space in a building: the suite number, the tenant, the square footage, the rent, and the dates the lease runs between. If you handle landlord work, it is the document you already keep, and it is usually in a spreadsheet or a PDF from the management company.

Put it in CRE Fuel and it stops being a document you look at. It becomes the thing that tells you who to call.

## Why it is worth the typing

The dates on a rent roll are the whole point. **A lease expiring in nine months is a listing, and a suite sitting empty is a listing right now.** Both are yours to win if you get there first.

Once the roll is in, those dates feed **Properties > Available spaces**, which gathers every empty and soon-to-be-empty space across all your buildings into one list.

<Info>
  Most brokers who use this run under 20 buildings and add one or two a month. At around 15 suites a building, entering one is a few minutes of typing — usually done once by whoever handles admin, and everyone on the team sees the result.
</Info>

## Add a rent roll

1. Open the property.
2. Find the **Rent Roll** card and click **Add a rent roll**.
3. Fill in the first space and click **Add space**.
4. Repeat for each suite.

**Only the unit number is required.** Everything else can wait, so you can list the suites first and come back for the leases when you have the paperwork in front of you.

## What each field is for

| Field | What to put in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Unit(s)** | The suite number. If one lease covers several suites, list them comma-separated — `100, 110`. |
| **Tenant** | The name on the lease. Leave it **blank if the space is vacant**. |
| **Area (SF)** | Square footage of the space. |
| **Rent** and **Rent basis** | The rent, and which unit you typed it in. |
| **Lease type** | Triple Net, Modified Gross, and so on. Leave it as **Not stated** if you do not know yet. |
| **Lease from / Lease to** | The dates the lease runs between. |
| **Recovery / CAM** | The monthly reimbursement, if the lease has one. |
| **Security deposit** | Held on the lease. |
| **Notes** | Renewal options, guarantees, anything you would scribble in the margin. |

### One lease can cover several suites

If a tenant holds two or three suites on a single lease, **enter it as one space** and list the units together: `620-05, 620-06, 620-07`. That is one lease with one rent and one expiry date, which is what it actually is. Splitting it into separate rows would mean inventing a rent and a square footage for each one.

### Rent: type it the way you have it

You do not have to convert anything. Pick the basis that matches your paperwork:

- **$ / month** — a monthly dollar amount
- **$ / year** — an annual dollar amount
- **$ / SF / month**
- **$ / SF / year**

CRE Fuel stores what you typed and works out the rest. The rent roll always shows you both the monthly figure and the annual rate per square foot, and because there is only ever one number underneath, they cannot drift apart.

<Warning>
  If you enter a rent, you must pick a basis. CRE Fuel will not guess. Assuming a yearly figure was monthly would show a rent twelve times too large, and you would have no way of knowing.
</Warning>

## What the closed card tells you

The Rent Roll card stays closed until you open it, because the summary line usually answers the question on its own:

> **Rent Roll** · 12 units · 8 leased, 3 vacant · 73% leased · $46,595/mo · next roll Jan 2027

If any lease has run past its end date, the card says so in orange. That is the most useful thing a rent roll can tell you, and it is easy to miss on paper.

### "Occupancy not stated"

Sometimes the card shows this instead of a percentage. It means CRE Fuel cannot work occupancy out honestly, and it will not print a number it cannot stand behind.

This happens when a space is marked as unknown, or when the spaces have no square footage to measure against — a pad site or a parking area, for example.

<Tip>
  If you would rather see a figure, open the card and give the unclear spaces a square footage or mark them leased or vacant. The percentage appears as soon as the building adds up.
</Tip>

## Vacant space belongs in the roll

It is tempting to only enter the leases. Do not.

**A vacant suite is a row too** — the unit number and its size, with the tenant left blank. Without it, the occupancy figure is wrong and the empty space never reaches your Available Spaces list, which is where it turns into work.

## Free rent is not the same as vacant

If a tenant is inside a free-rent period, enter their rent as **0** and keep the tenant name. The space is leased and occupied, and it counts that way in every figure.

Leaving the tenant name blank would mark the suite empty and put it on your available list, where you might market space that is not actually available.

## Keeping it current

Open the card, click the pencil on any row, change what moved, and save. Rent steps, a renewal, a tenant moving out — all of it is a row edit.

When a tenant vacates, clear the tenant name and the lease dates. The suite moves to vacant and appears on **Available spaces** straight away.

<Info>
  Deleting a space removes it from the building and from your available list. If a tenant left but you still want the history, clear the tenant instead of deleting the row.
</Info>
